The Bulwark at Gate 12:
The 2001 Attack & The 2002 Crisis

At 11:40 AM on 13 December 2001, five heavily armed militants breached the perimeter of India’s Parliament in an Ambassador car. Nine vigilant defenders laid down their lives to safeguard the sovereign legislature—igniting Operation Parakram, the historic POTA joint parliament enactment, and a landmark constitutional trial in 2002.

Constitutional & Evidentiary Precedents

Analytical Jurisprudence

The legal proceedings born out of the attack altered Indian anti-terror jurisprudence, digital surveillance admissibility, and due-process standards for decades.

Indian Evidence Act Benchmarks

Call Detail Records (CDRs) as Substantive Evidence

The 2002 trial was India’s first major litigation where cellphone tower triangulation, SIM card purchase ledgers, and telephonic intercepts were pivotal. The prosecution tied the five deceased attackers to conspirators via intercepted satellite and mobile numbers.

// Historical Precedent Established:
• Special Court admitted printouts of computerized call logs under Section 65.
• The Supreme Court later established strict evidentiary protocols for certifying secondary electronic media under Section 65B, setting the stage for subsequent cyber law reform.
Statutory Tension

The Admissibility of Confessions to Police (Section 32 POTA)

Unlike the Indian Evidence Act—which excludes confessions made directly to police officers—POTA Section 32 permitted confessions recorded by an officer not below Superintendent rank to be admitted as evidence against the confessor.

// Historical Precedent Established:
• The Supreme Court scrutinized the timing, voluntariness, and absence of independent legal counsel during confession recording.
• Confessions were strictly barred from being used as substantive evidence against co-accused, directly influencing the repeal of POTA in 2004.
Constitutional Safeguards

Appellate Reversal: The Acquittals of Geelani and Navjot Sandhu

The 2002 Special Court had originally convicted all four individuals brought to trial, handing down multiple capital sentences. However, the Delhi High Court (2003) and Supreme Court (2005) held that suspicion—no matter how grave—cannot substitute for legal proof.

// Historical Precedent Established:
• S.A.R. Geelani was acquitted of all charges due to insufficient phone intercept corroboration.
• Navjot Sandhu (Afsan Guru) was acquitted of conspiracy charges, upholding civil liberties standards in anti-terror adjudication.
